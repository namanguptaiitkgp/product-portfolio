Guaranteed demand — but no capital to fund the trade.

Ninjacart, India's largest domestic agri platform, launched Ninja Global to move into international agri trade, and I was brought in to build it 0→1 with full P&L ownership.The real mandate wasn't a feature — it was finding product-market fit in a market we didn't yet serve. My first hypothesis was a marketplace play: match importers and exporters across borders, verify trade partners, and earn a commission on matchmaking. It onboarded well, buttraders wouldn't pay for discovery — matchmaking wasn't the real pain. Talking to traders in India, Malaysia and Vietnam, the urgent need was clear: capital. Exporters pay suppliers up front but wait 30–90 days for the importer's balance, and banks won't finance perishable, MSME-scale trade. That capital-timing gap became the product.

Two personas — the same broken capital cycle.

We bet on networking. The data said financing.

Networking onboarded but wouldn't monetise — no one paid commission for matchmaking. Interviews in India / Malaysia / Vietnam said the pain was capital → I re-pointed the roadmap, then sequenced the build by effort vs impact under hard constraints.

One modular credit flow. Onboarding to a real limit.

One flow, reused across PODs — onboarding → disbursal → repayment.

Getting the user and setting the limit

Clear entry — Credit is very important part of offerings that the company provides and its very crucial to have it straight up on a clean spot, without having the user navigate too much.

This approach has been followed across Ninjacart platforms enabling the credit

Friendly, trustworthy landing

Straight after onboarding you land on a focused home — only the parts that matter, the clutter stripped out.

Know your potential limit — This was done because it was observed by AB testing that showing your limit at the end of onboarding made the users feel a sense of disappointment and gave a sense of untrustworthiness because most of the times the limit that a user gets allocated based on a rule engine considering multiple factors usually always ends up being less than the maximum possible amount that can be taken.

Therefore, resetting and showing the potential limit based on the income level sets the expectation of the user straight right from the start helping build a sense of trust and transparency for the user.

Let's take a selfie

Liveness + selfie KYC in seconds — the lightest possible identity step before we ever ask for a document.

Knowing the applicant

Two paths: self-upload for the savvy, and an assisted flow run by a TradeDesk agent for users who aren't.

Few more business details

A few business documents and details let us assess the business and arrive at the real limit and credit-worthiness. We split the flow two ways — self-upload for the savvy, and an assisted, agent-led upload powered by the TradeDesk for everyone else.

Getting the last details, gracefully

Dynamic drop-off screens adapt if the user leaves mid-way and nudge them back to finish. The address auto-fetches from the map instead of being typed, and bank details are confirmed before disbursal — fewer fields, fewer errors.

Sign and takeaway the cash

The journey is done and the limit has been set. The user can just sign and takeaway the money with a few clicks.

Your potential limit — up front

A/B-won: showing the potential limit at the start built trust, where revealing it only at the end felt like a let-down.

From a clean entry to a real, approved limit — underwriting done.

Financing a trade needs trade-specific docs. So we stopped chasing them.

A loan request rides on a specific trade — and every trade carries its own documents.

Optimizing for speed — The flow was designed with keeping in mind the primary focus which is to help the user take a loan quickly. The user should be able to take a loan as quickly as possible without having to think too much about the steps.

The chase lived in chat

A customer raises a loan request against a trade — but ops receives a thin request, realises something's missing, and has no direct line to the customer. The chase runs through sales, documents land scattered across chat threads, and nothing routes them back to ops.

Ask up front — inline with the request

The first screen offers one clear choice — “Yes, I'll upload it myself” or “No, I need some assistance” — and the supporting docs are requested then and there, with explicit guidance on what's needed and why. The case arrives underwriting-ready instead of triggering a chase.

Incomplete? Ops fires a templated request

For requests that still come in thin, ops sends a predefined document checklist + message template straight to the customer — “loan on hold pending X” — replacing the sales → chat → ticket detour. Docs land directly in a structured pipeline ops pulls from, not buried in a thread.

Every document tagged to a party

The Ninjacart agent assigns each request as a task tagged to a specific member of the trade — packing pictures, CRO docs, financials all land against the right party. A clean, tagged pipeline into ops instead of an untracked thread — the moat that cut hold time by ~75%.

Every charge, shown before you commit

A loan calculator lays out the borrow amount, tenure, interest and processing fee up front — and reviewing it never touches your credit score. The customer sees the exact total disbursal before a single click, then gets the money in an instant.

From a chase across chat to a request that arrives underwriting-ready — and a loan taken in full transparency.

Repayment — every due, one surface.

A digital-only lender can't knock on doors — so the repayment experience makes paying on time the path of least resistance.

The first months taught us the model — then we compounded it.

Early disbursals surfaced three failure modes. Each insight became a fix, each fix became a system — and that's what let us scale disbursals 10× while cutting NPA 6% → 2%.
